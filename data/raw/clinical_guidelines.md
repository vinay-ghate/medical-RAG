# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Evidence-Based Summaries

Source: https://www.cdc.gov/antimicrobial-resistance/guidance/index.html  
Date: 2024-01-15  
Doc Type: clinical_guideline  

## Section: Antibiotic Stewardship and Acute Otitis Media (CDC/AAP)
Diagnostic accuracy is critical to prevent overprescribing. Uncomplicated acute otitis media in children without severe signs or symptoms may initially be observed with safety-net antibiotic prescriptions. First-line antibacterial therapy for confirmed bacterial acute otitis media remains high-dose amoxicillin (80 to 90 mg/kg/day divided in 2 doses), provided the child is not allergic to penicillin and has not received amoxicillin within the previous 30 days. In patients with immediate hypersensitivity to penicillins, non-beta-lactam alternatives such as macrolides (azithromycin or clarithromycin) or clindamycin should be evaluated.

## Section: Blood Pressure Management and Target Thresholds (AHA/ACC)
Adults with confirmed hypertension (systolic BP >= 130 mmHg or diastolic BP >= 80 mmHg) should initiate lifestyle modifications coupled with pharmacotherapy when 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk is >= 10%. Initial first-line antihypertensive agents include thiazide diuretics, calcium channel blockers (CCBs), and ACE inhibitors (such as lisinopril) or ARBs. Dual-agent therapy of different classes is recommended for patients with Stage 2 hypertension (BP >= 140/90 mmHg). ACE inhibitors are strongly recommended for patients with diabetes and albuminuria to slow progression of chronic kidney disease.

## Section: Type 2 Diabetes Glycemic Control and Renal Safeguards (ADA Standards of Care)
Metformin remains the foundational first-line pharmacological therapy for comprehensive type 2 diabetes management alongside lifestyle modifications, provided there are no contraindications. Renal function must be monitored via estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) prior to initiation and at least annually thereafter. Metformin is contraindicated if eGFR drops below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, and dose reduction or cautious monitoring is mandated when eGFR is between 30 and 45 mL/min/1.73 m2. SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 receptor agonists are indicated as add-on therapy for patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease.
